Agent skills for people who'd rather be told asked.

Your agent will make a hundred product decisions today. It won't mention most of them. Plenty of those were yours to make.

These skills make it ask you first, show you the calls it made on its own, and hand the ones that matter back. They're what I use every day, refined on real work.

For PMs, engineers, founders and designers: anyone whose agent builds faster than they can decide.

Why these exist

Every gap you leave, your agent fills. Quietly.

Ask for something big and your agent starts immediately. No questions, no brief, no idea how you'll judge the result. Every gap it hits it fills with a guess, and a guess about what to build is a product decision. You find out which ones it got wrong once the work is built on top of them.

A good product person does the opposite. They ask first. They come back with a goal you can correct. They say plainly which calls are theirs and which are yours. That's all these skills do: they make the agent work that way, so the decisions that matter stay with the person who owns them.

The skills

Two are ready to install. Two are in the workshop.

Each one takes a job a product person does by hand and teaches it to your agent. They work in Claude Code, and they're written in plain English: no code required to use them, or to read them.

  1. 1.

    Context is Queen

    Your agent decides what “done” means the moment you stop talking. This one asks instead. It interviews you a question at a time, proposes a goal for you to correct, holds itself to that goal, and lists every call it made without you when you ask.

    Turning Research Into Strategy

    You have call transcripts, a painpoint tracker and a deck claiming eleven customers asked for the thing. This gives up the easy assertion. It counts the evidence under a rule you set first, opens the product and tries it, and traces every number back to the person who said it.

    Best Use of My Time

    Decisions pile up in the chat and your attention pays for it. This turns them into plain questions you can answer in a few words — the recommendation already made, the evidence beside it, and never a question built on a guess. Most fundamental first, so the call that decides the most other work comes to you first.

Get started

Context is Queen, in about a minute.

Open Claude Code and paste this into the chat. “Globally” makes it available in every project, not just this one.

› Install the Context is Queen skill globally from github.com/hgambas/think-like-a-pm

Prefer the plugin system? Run /plugin marketplace add hgambas/think-like-a-pm, then install context-is-queen from the /plugin menu. That version also sets up the session-start nudge, so fresh sessions offer the briefing on their own.

  1. 1.
    Ask for something complex, in your own words

    “Rework our onboarding so people actually finish setup.” No special syntax needed. The briefing starts on its own, and simple asks skip it.

  2. 2.
    Answer the questions, one at a time

    Three to eight of them, covering only what your agent can't research for itself.

  3. 3.
    Correct the goal it proposes

    Then it works to that goal, and checks itself against it before claiming anything is done.

“Work aimed at a goal you've corrected is simply better work — and you stay the decision-maker on your own project.”

Who's behind this

I'm Hiba Ganta. I'm the product person and the designer; my agent is my engineer. I don't read code, which means I can't catch a wrong turn by reading the diff. I have to catch it in how the work gets decided.

Every skill here started as a rule I kept repeating to my own agent until it was worth writing down properly.
