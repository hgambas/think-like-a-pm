# debrief — unpack one call into six buckets

## Purpose

Turn a founder call, working session, or customer conversation into a structured record of what was actually said, while it is fresh.

## When to use it

Right after any call that produced commitments, decisions, or a problem statement. Also retroactively, on any recorded call that was never processed.

## Inputs

A transcript or recording notes for one call (grade it via `discovery` if it hasn't been). If none exists, say so and stop — do not reconstruct a call from memory.

## How it works

Extract the call into six buckets. Quote verbatim where the source grade allows it; paraphrase and say so where it doesn't.

1. **Explicit commitments** — who promised what, by when.
2. **Inputs you are blocked on** — things the other person holds that work is waiting for.
3. **Their problem, stated precisely** — in their own words where possible.
4. **Decisions actually reached** — dated, attributed.
5. **Open questions** — raised but not resolved.
6. **Implied work nobody claimed** — tasks the conversation assumes will happen, with every inference marked as inference (e.g. "*Inference: this assumes someone builds the export*").

Then **separate what they asked for from what they are stuck on.** These often diverge — the ask may arrive framed as prioritisation when what is actually missing is a mapping between data, tools, and jobs to be done. Record both readings side by side, and take the stated ask as a hypothesis, not a work order.

Append to the decision log: decisions from bucket 4, questions from bucket 5, and waiting-on entries from bucket 2.

## A healthy run produces

A one-page debrief with the six buckets filled in, every inference tagged, and a closing line naming the gap (if any) between the stated ask and the diagnosed problem.

## Pitfalls

- **Recording only the outcome of a disagreement.** Record the pushback and the resolution; the reasoning is the reusable part.
- **Treating the loudest ask as the problem.** If the ask and the stuck-point diverge, the divergence is the finding — surface it, don't pick one silently.
- **Using this on a pile of calls at once.** One call per debrief. A corpus goes to `evidence`.
