# discovery — collect and grade the raw material

## Purpose

Intake. Gather everything the method mines, and stamp each source with a confidence grade as it arrives, so the evidence base is labeled from the start.

## When to use it

First, before anything else — or whenever new material arrives mid-project (a new batch of calls, an updated deck).

## Before you ask for anything: find out where it lives

Before gathering anything: if no finish condition has been agreed yet, settle it with the user first — one sentence on what the finished work must be able to do (see The Finish Condition in the skill's main file).

Then ask where the material lives — the tool, not the file. "Where do the call notes sit? Notion, a Drive folder, a board somewhere?" Most research has two or three homes, and the user knows all of them without having to look.

Now work out what you can reach. **Check your own tool list, not their workspace.** A connected home shows up as a tool you can call, named for Notion, Google Drive, Figma, Slack, Linear and so on. Reading that list is the entire check — you open nothing to perform it.

Being able to call a tool is not proof it will work: the account may not be signed in, or the permission may not cover the space you need. Treat the list as your best guess. If the harness gives you no way to tell, or a first call fails, say so plainly and ask.

Each home is now in one of two states, and they are handled differently.

### When the tool is connected

You still do not go looking. **Read only what the user hands you.** Never search a connected workspace for research-shaped material: a keyword sweep drags in the other project's notes, the half-written doc and the thing they meant to delete, and you would end up grading material they never chose to give you.

What being connected buys them is that handing something over is a link instead of a copy-paste. Say that plainly, and ask for links:

> "Notion's connected, so just paste the link to the page and I'll read it — no need to copy the text across."

Make each ask specific enough to answer. "Send me your research" gets nothing back; "which page has the customer calls on it?" gets a link. Where a home has obvious sub-locations — a teamspace, a shared drive, a project — ask which one, and take the link to it.

Two traps once you can open things:

- **Links inside a page.** A page with fourteen links in it is one source, not fifteen. Follow a link only when the user pointed at it, or when it plainly is the material — a page called "Call — Jules, 14 May" sitting in an index they gave you. List the rest and ask.
- **Files you can see but were not offered.** If a linked folder holds more than they mentioned, list what is in it and ask which items are in scope. Never quietly widen the corpus.

### When the tool is not connected

Say it once, with the cost and the fix in the same breath, then carry on regardless:

> "Miro isn't connected here, so I can't open the board. Send me an export and keep the groups — a flat list of stickies loses which cluster each one sat in. Either way the cost is that a quote can't be re-checked against the original later. Connecting Miro in your connector settings would fix that. Export, or connect?"

Three rules:

- **Once per home, not once per file.** Say it when the tool first comes up. Do not re-pitch it on the fourth pasted document.
- **Never block on it.** Take the paste, the export, the screenshot. A skill that refuses to start until something is configured is a skill people stop opening.
- **Log it.** Put it in the decision log as an open item, with what it costs: "Miro not connected — board arrived as an export, so quotes cannot be re-checked against the original." If it starts to hurt later, the record is already there.

Setting up a connector is the user's own action inside their app's settings. You cannot do it for them, and you must never imply that you have. Name where the setting is if you know this harness; say "in your connector settings" if you don't.

### Homes worth naming out loud

Ask about these by name rather than waiting for the user to remember them. The third column is what to do when you can't open one.

| Where it lives | What is usually in it | If you can't open it |
|---|---|---|
| Notion, Confluence | Call notes, painpoint trackers, research databases | Export to text, or paste page by page |
| Google Drive, Dropbox, SharePoint | Recordings, transcript files, spreadsheets, decks | Upload the files, or paste |
| FigJam, Miro, Mural | Workshop boards, journey maps, sticky-note clusters | Export the board with its groups intact — a flat sticky list loses them |
| Figma | The prototype and the product as designed — this feeds `teardown` | A screenshot walkthrough of each screen |
| Slack, Teams | Customer channels, feedback threads, the complaint someone dropped in passing | Paste the thread |
| Linear, Jira, Trello | The backlog | Export or paste |
| Granola, Otter, Fathom, Gong | Auto-notes and machine transcripts | Export each call |

## What to collect

Ask for the material one item at a time, using a question card per item where the harness supports it. Do not demand everything up front; take what exists and record what is missing as an open question.

1. **Call transcripts.** For each, record who the call was with, their role, and the date.
2. **Auto-generated notes** (Granola, Otter, Gemini and similar). Flag them for wording verification before any quote from them is used outside the team.
3. **The research tracker** — painpoint lists, feedback boards, spreadsheets.
4. **Workshop boards** — FigJam, Miro, or wherever the session was run. See the pre-grouped stamp below.
5. **The backlog**, if one exists.
6. **Existing strategy material** — decks, strategy docs, prior versions. These are inputs to be audited, not truths to inherit.
7. **The product** — a URL, a test account, or whatever is closest to real (a demo, the founder's spreadsheet).

## Grading

As each source lands, grade it:

| Grade | Meaning | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| T | A transcript the user supplied or recorded themselves | Strongest. Verbatim quotes allowed. |
| G | Auto-notes plus a machine transcript | Verify wording before any external use. |
| G-notes | Notes only, no transcript | Weakest. Paraphrase only — no verbatim exists. |

State the mix openly in the intake summary (e.g. "14 of 20 calls are machine-transcribed, 1 is notes-only").

**One extra stamp: pre-grouped.** It sits alongside the grade, not inside it. The grade says how far you can trust the words; the stamp says whose arrangement they arrived in.

A workshop board comes in **whole, with its clusters intact**. Somebody grouped those stickies in the room, with context you don't have, and flattening the board to get loose notes throws that judgment away. So the board arrives as one source, themes and all, graded on its words like anything else — stickies are notes, so usually G-notes.

The cost is that somebody's grouping now sits inside the evidence, and a count drawn from it is partly a count of how the stickies were arranged. The stamp is what stops that going invisible: it travels with the source, and any later count leaning on it says so — "9 mentions, 6 of them from one pre-grouped board."

## A healthy run produces

A one-page intake summary: every source listed with its owner, date and grade; any source stamped pre-grouped; the grade mix stated; which homes were reachable and which were not; and a short list of what is missing, each entry naming who could supply it.

## Pitfalls

- **Waiting for a complete corpus before starting.** Take what exists; missing material becomes open questions in the decision log, not a reason to stall.
- **Searching a connected workspace because you can.** Access is not an invitation. The user names what is in scope.
- **Selling the connector.** One mention per home, with its cost attached. After that it's nagging, and the work still hasn't started.
- **Treating a deck as evidence.** Decks arrive as claims to check. If you're about to quote one as fact, you wanted `validate`.
- **Grading by effort instead of provenance.** A beautifully formatted notes-only doc is still G-notes.
