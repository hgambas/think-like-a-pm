# The analysts

A standing roster of brand and culture writers, consulted on every `read` and
credited wherever their thinking shows up.

## What they are — and what they are not

These are **secondary sources with a point of view**. An expert reading of the
room, already interpreted, by someone with a career and a thesis.

The field — real people, their own words, unmediated — is a different kind of
evidence, and the two never get merged. "People are saying X" and "Ana Andjelic
argues X" are not the same claim, and quietly turning the second into the first
is the most respectable-looking way this skill could lie to you.

So: **their thinking stays visibly separate from what ordinary people said**,
always, and it always carries their name.

Where they and the field disagree, that goes in the first reply as one of the
findings — it is often the most interesting line in the whole read. An analyst
who agrees with the field, or who handed you a frame that shaped how you saw it,
comes out in the second reply. Either way, name them at the point of use rather
than in a list at the bottom. See [How to hand it back](read.md#how-to-hand-it-back).

## When to consult them

- Every `read`, after the field passes are done — never before. Go to the field
  first with a clean head, so their frames don't decide what you notice.
- Match on **beat**, not on vibe. Consult the two or three whose territory
  actually covers this topic.
- If none of them cover it, say so: *"none of the roster works this beat."*
  That's a better answer than a forced quote, and it tells the user where the
  roster has a hole.
- On a re-read or a one-phrase question, consult only if a specific beat is
  directly on point. Usually skip.

## How to fetch their latest

Window: same as the read (last three months by default). Look for what they've
published **recently**, not their greatest hits — the roster exists to keep the
read current, and a five-year-old essay is baseline, not field.

- **Substack:** `<url>/archive?sort=new` for recent posts, `<url>/feed` for RSS.
- **Ghost** (`culture.ghost.io`): `/rss` or the front page.
- **Personal sites:** the articles index given below.

Paywalls are real. If a post is paywalled and you can only see the opening,
**say that** — use what's public, mark it partial, and never infer the argument
behind the wall.

## Attribution — non-negotiable

1. **Credited at the point of use, not in a bibliography at the bottom.** Name,
   piece title, date, link, right where the idea is being used.
2. **Coinages carry the name permanently.** If they named a thing, the name
   travels with it every time. "Vibe shift" is Sean Monahan's. "The age of
   average" is Alex Murrell's. You don't get to adopt the term and drop the
   person.
3. **Frameworks get credited, not just quotes.** This is the one that matters.
   The risk isn't quoting someone — it's absorbing their *lens*, then presenting
   that way of seeing as your own analysis. When a finding is shaped by
   someone's framework rather than lifted from their words, say so: *"read
   through Douglas Holt's cultural branding frame —"*.
4. **Don't put words in their mouth.** If you're extending their idea to a case
   they didn't write about, that's your inference on their frame. Label it.

## The roster

Grouped by beat, which is what makes them routable. All URLs verified live
2026-08-07.

**The "best of" links are calibration, not material.** They're there so you can
recognise what this person's good thinking sounds like and tell it from their
filler. Don't quote them into a read and don't let them shape the finding — a
celebrated essay is an anchor pulling you backwards, and the roster exists to
keep the read *current*. Cite recent work.

### Status, class and positioning

**Eugene Healey** — https://eugenehealey.substack.com/
Status economics of post-luxury consumers; the collapse of mass-middle
positioning. *Best of:* [The Death of the Middle](https://eugenehealey.substack.com/p/the-death-of-the-middle)

**W. David Marx** — https://culture.ghost.io/
Status-driven theory of cultural change; author of *Status and Culture*.
*Best of:* [Only Fads: A Culture (and Economy) of Labubu](https://culture.ghost.io/only-fads-a-culture-and-economy-of-labubu/)

**Sean Monahan** — https://www.8ball.report/
Coined "vibe shift"; emergent status archetypes.
*Best of:* [Strivers, stoners, scammers, and builders](https://www.8ball.report/p/strivers-stoners-scammers-and-builders)

**Ana Andjelic** — https://andjelicaaa.substack.com/
Cultural capital treated as a financial asset class.
*Best of:* [Cultural derivatives](https://andjelicaaa.substack.com/p/cultural-derivatives)

Originator of Cultural Branding Theory.

## Known holes in the roster

Say so plainly in a read when the topic falls in one of these:

- Heavily regulated or clinical territory — health, finance, legal
- Non-Western markets and non-English-language culture
- B2B and enterprise buying culture
- Anything where the audience is defined by a condition or a life event rather
  than by taste

The roster is strong on taste, status and consumer meaning, and thin
everywhere else. That's a real limit, not a gap to paper over with the nearest
available name.
