# The interview

Ask the user what only the user knows. Everything else is yours to research.

## The one rule that matters most

**One question at a time — never a batch.** A batch of five questions gets
skimmed and half-answered, because it's easy to get lazy and not take your
time. One question gets a real answer. Wait for each answer before asking
the next; let answers change what you ask.

## What to cover

Work through whatever is genuinely unsettled from this list — skip what the
ask already answers:

- **Gaps** in the original instructions — surface them, never fill them
  with a guess.
- **What it replaces** — if this work supersedes something that already
  exists: what's the old thing, who still depends on it, and what's the
  transition — migrate, dual-run, redirect, archive, delete, or leave
  untouched? Unresolved? Mark it an open call, and don't build
  replacement work that assumes an answer.
- **Effort** — where they want more of it spent, and where less.
- **Outcome** — what they expect at the end, and how they will test or
  evaluate it.
- **Their role** — which decisions they want brought back to them, and
  which ones you own.
- **Task type** — is this planning only, planning and building, or
  something else?
- **Help** — does the job need more than one person or helper on it, and
  if so, doing what?

## How to ask

- Lead with your recommendation when you have one, so the user reacts
  instead of authoring from scratch.
- Offer real options where they exist — a choice is easier to answer than
  a blank.
- Plain, everyday language. No jargon. Assume the user doesn't read code.

## When you're done

Stop when you're confident you understand the job — not when the list is
exhausted. Expect three to eight questions for most tasks. Then move on:
in a full briefing, that means [set-goal.md](set-goal.md).

The user can also end it at any time: "enough", "just build it", or
"skip the questions" stops the interview immediately — propose the goal
from what you have and flag the unknowns as unknowns.

## Pitfalls

- **Batching.** The whole point dies. One at a time.
- **Asking what you could research.** The user's time goes only to
  questions only they can answer.
- **Accepting vague answers.** "Standard" and "normal" are not answers —
  ask what standard looks like here. Specificity is the whole point.
- **Interrogating.** Past ten questions, propose the goal and let their
  corrections finish the job.
